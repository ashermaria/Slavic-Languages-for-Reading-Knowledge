Introduction

Reading a languages consists of just two elements: grammar and vocab. The good news is that, while we might think of learning a language from scratch as a daunting task, there is actually a limit on the amount of "necessary" information you need to become proficient in reading.

With vocab, to feel relatively proficient you need to familiarize yourself with ˜2,000 words. At this level you should have relative ease reading everyday writing like news articles, academic writing, and TV subtitles.

I have a sample of the first 2,000 words here. Study these words in any order that is most useful for you.

To read more complicated texts (e.g., literature, poetry, legal documents) efficiently you will need at least 5,000. For the next 3,000, you can either search for an official B2-C1 vocab list and work through those, or you can simply just add words to your studying as you come across them (I much prefer the latter because vocab needs become much more specific at this point).

If you are new to a language, you might view its grammar as a collection of horribly dense arbitrary rules that only get more complicated as you progress. However, while most languages have advanced grammatical elements, 99% of what you will need can be summed up in the following list:
