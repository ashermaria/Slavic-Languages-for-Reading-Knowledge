How to Study a Language for Reading Knowledge

Introduction

Reading a languages consists of just two elements: grammar and vocab. The good news is that, while we might think of learning a language from scratch as a daunting task, there is actually a limit on the amount of "necessary" information you need to become proficient in reading.

With vocab, to feel relatively proficient you need to familiarize yourself with ˜2,000 words. At this level you should have relative ease reading everyday writing like news articles, academic writing, and TV subtitles.

I have a sample of the first 2,000 words here. Study these words in any order that is most useful for you.

To read more complicated texts (e.g., literature, poetry, legal documents) efficiently you will need at least 5,000. For the next 3,000, you can either search for an official B2-C1 vocab list and work through those, or you can simply just add words to your studying as you come across them (I much prefer the latter because vocab needs become much more specific at this point).

If you are new to a language, you might view its grammar as a collection of horribly dense arbitrary rules that only get more complicated as you progress. However, while most languages have advanced grammatical elements, 99% of what you will need can be summed up in the following list:

  1. Nouns
  2. Verbs
    1. Present
    2. Past
    3. Future
    4. Negation
  3. Participles
  4. Adjectives
  5. Comparatives
  6. Superlatives
  7. Pronouns
  8. Determiners
  9. Conjunctions
  10. Prepositions
  11. Numerals, dates, time

    12. and depending on the language

  12. Gender
  13. Formality
  14. Articles
  15. Cases
  16. Demonstratives
  17. Passive Verbs
  18. Reflexive Verbs
  19. Subjunctive Mood
  20. Conditional Mood

Of course this list cannot be exhaustive, but for the majority of commonly studied languages the above elements are what you will need.

While this may seem like a lot, once you memorize the rules associated with each of these elements there will be very little more to learn to become reading proficient. I have spent over 1,000 hours of my life studying vocab using Anki and it has enabled me to confidently read in over 12 different languages. I currently have about 25,000 cards in my deck across my languages (not even including Russian which I read fluently in). With the three study tricks I have below, you will be able to master the majority of these in a few months if not weeks.

General Study Tips

  1. Study 25 Words a Day

    2. By using a repetition-based card memorization app like Anki you can make sure that you have a good balance between learning new cards and reviewing old ones, with errors caught and fixed. When learning a new language, I like to begin with 25 words a day. This way the review portion does not become overbearing while I still making massive progress. If your goal is 2,000 words, then you can reach that in just 80 days of consistent study. I have provided advanced Anki studying tips at the bottom of this page.

    Anki is free to download online and costs $25 on the app store. It is a worthwile investment to have it so easily accesible. I use it on the bus, while on walks, and when watching TV.

  2. Study by Root

    3. Study words by shared etymology. For example, if you were studying English, you might put the words "mother," "motherly," and "maternal," "motherland," and "grandmother" together. Then, you might put "land" and "grand" as the next words in your sequence. This way you only have to learn one root and get (in this case) five words out of it. This is also helpful for studying word formations as you will quickly pick up patterns (e.g., make certain adjectives by simply adding "-ly" to the end). Even though "motherly" is not a very common word and will not be on most beginner or even intermediate vocab lists, by memorizing it you have another connection made to the word that you ultimately need to remember: mother. You should also include key phrases the word is in after you add the word. For example, with the word "corner," you might immediately add "on the corner" as a card after. Also, this should be obvious but if the language is gendered, you should always include all common gendered variations of the term (this mostly happens for professions like teacher).

  3. Turn Grammar into Vocab

Specific Anki Tips

  1. Think of your Anki deck as your personal dictionary. Whenever you are reading a text always have it open in another tab so refer to, the search feature is much quicker to navigate than online dictionaries, and this way you can always check if you have seen the word before. Always add new words when you see them, even if they seem niche. Often the words you least expect are the ones that resurface again and again. You should feel a sense of ownership over the words you put in your dictionary, they are YOUR words now.
  2. Record your own voice saying the words when you create the vocab cards so that you have auditory alongside visual stimulus. If the language you are learning does not have standard pronunciation, then you shoudl always confirm pronunciation either using Wiktionary.org or Forvo.com
  3. one of the most useful aspects of Anki is the fields feature. When creating an Anki card, you can add fields to add additional information to the back of the card that is key for the target word or grammatical element. I have found incedibly useful application for this in
    1. examples usages of the vocab or grammar
    2. highlighting verb conjugations or irregular forms
    3. showing verb mood (especially important for many Slavic languages)
    4. showing which syllable stress falls on
  4. use tags to mark words that fall into specific categories. For example, you might add an "irregular" tag to all of your irregular verbs so that you can easily check which of YOUR words fall into this category
