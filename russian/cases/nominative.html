Nominative Case

The nominative case gets its name from the concept of "naming" (Latin nōminātīvus (“pertaining to naming, nominative”)). Therefore, this is the case of the grammatical subject

Consonant Rules

In order to determine how plurals are constructed in Russian, we need to first go over one more spelling rule

All consonants in Russian are either hard or soft, which produce different sounds

A consonant is HARD if followed by the following, А, О, У, Ы, Э, Ъ, another consonant, or nothing

A consonant is SOFT if followed by the following, Я, Ё, Ю, И, Е, Ь, or Й (depends)

Examples

том - hard

столь - soft

Usage

The nominative case is primarily used for three purposes, either to establish the subject of a sentence, "there is" statements, or in statements denoting direct equivalence (Baxter is a dog). Unlike in English, you do not need to capitalize the first-person singular pronoun. Important: Russian does not use articles, so you never have to specify if it is "the" or "a" something, the meaning is implied based on context. Examples:

я–собака (I am a dog) (one typically uses em-dashes to form statements of equivalence)

я голоден (I am hungry)

это Юлия (this is Yulia)

есть собака здесь (there is a dog here)

там есть группа (a band is there)

Irregular plurals

There are various nouns that have irregular plural forms. These are often common nouns and must be memorized. Examples:

дом → дома́

брат → братья́

друг → друзья

человек → лю́ди

ребёнок → де́ти

отец → отцы́

мать→ ма́тери

дочь → до́чери

IMPORTANT: when declining these words, you always treat мать and дочь as if they are their archaic forms "матерь" and "дочерь," that is why their plural forms differ so greatly

Indeclinables

Many foreign words are also indeclinable in Russian, meaning that no matter how you use them they always maintain the same form. Examples:

радио

метро

кенгуру

такси

Nominative Case Pronouns

Here are the nominative pronouns. The neuter pronoun can only refer to inanimate objects. Refering to someone as "оно" is considered dehumanizing. Russians have several ways of identify non-binary persons, including using они.
